Ribbon control UI

Ribbon lays every window side by side on one sideways strip. These five surfaces sit on top of it. Each one answers a single question, shows only what you need for it, then gets out of the way.

Surface 1 · while ⌃⌥ is held

The strip

A to-scale map of the current desktop, docked above the Dock. Tiles keep their true widths, so you recognise your layout by its shape before you read a word.

Or hold Control on your keyboard. Click a tile to jump, drag one to reorder, ⌃⌥← → to move between columns.

Where am I, at a glance. Columns on screen are bright, the ones off to the sides fade back, and your window is the one raised tile with the ring. The caption adds only the title and “2/6”. Tiles are neutral, so the app icons are the only colour.

Surface 2 · after every command

Action feedback

A short confirmation that stays for about 1.8 seconds. Pressing the same key again updates the toast in place, so ten taps of the width key never pile up ten toasts.

Enter 220 msRises 8 pt and scales from 96 % while fading in.
Update in place 160 msText crossfades, width and diagram morph, a 3 % bump says “again”, and the 1.8 s timer restarts.
Exit 180 msAfter 1.8 s with no new command it fades and sinks 4 pt. A new command mid-exit reverses it.

Why B. For an ADHD brain the shape does the work: a column filling to ⅔ registers before the words do. B also stays as quiet as A for plain messages like “Moved left”, and Reduce Motion turns every move into a 120 ms crossfade.

Surface 3 · on a tabbed column

The tab bar

Each tab is its own glass capsule sitting directly on the window, and the bar is exactly as wide as the column. Tabs split that width between them and never reserve empty slots.

Surface 4 · ⌃⌥Space

Command bar

Windows and actions in one list. Type a few letters to jump anywhere or run anything without remembering which shortcut does it.

Try “wid 75”, “notes” or “desk”. Arrow keys move, Return runs, Escape closes.

It teaches you to need it less. With an empty search it suggests your neighbours and the likeliest actions. Windows carry ⌘-number quick picks, and actions show their real shortcut so you learn it by repetition.

States

Surface 5 · ⌃⌥B

Focus mode

Everything except the window you’re in is blurred and dimmed, and that window stays in the middle of the screen. Nothing closes, and leaving focus mode keeps the view where it is.

Click any blurred window and the cutout glides to it.
Recommendation

What I’d ship

Ship the strip as shown: docked above the Dock, visible only while ⌃⌥ is held, drawn to scale as slim neutral tiles where off-screen columns fade and only your window is raised and ringed, because a map you recognise by shape answers “where am I” faster than any label. Pair it with toast direction B, the glass pill with a live diagram, and fold that toast into the strip’s caption line whenever the strip is open, so Ribbon never shows two surfaces at once. Keep the tab bar as separate glass capsules with a single raised, neutral active tab and the stretch morph, and let the whole bar recede when its column loses focus. The command bar is the safety net for everything you can’t remember, and its shortcut chips slowly make it unnecessary. Focus mode stays a single reversible veil with a concentric cutout. That leaves exactly one accent, the focus ring, doing all the “you are here” work, which is the calm, predictable baseline an ADHD brain can trust on a Tuesday morning.
